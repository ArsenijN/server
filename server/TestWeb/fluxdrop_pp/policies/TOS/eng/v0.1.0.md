## 3. Storage and quotas

### 3.1 Quota

Each account has a storage quota that adjusts automatically to the free space
on the server:

| Server free space | Quota per account |
|---|---|
| More than 500 GB | 100 GB |
| 200 – 500 GB | 50 GB (default) |
| 100 – 200 GB | 35 GB |
| Less than 100 GB | 10 GB |

If the quota goes down while you are using more than the new limit, your
files are **not deleted**. You just can't upload more until you are under the
limit again. Your current quota and usage are shown in your profile. The
operator may set a fixed quota for individual accounts by arrangement.

Files in the trash bin do not count towards your quota.

### 3.2 File size and uploads

A single file can be up to **10 GB**. Large files are uploaded in resumable
parts. Unfinished uploads are removed after 48 hours without activity.

### 3.3 Trash bin

Deleted files go to the trash bin, where you can restore them:

- **Standard:** kept for **30 days**, then permanently deleted.
- **Low disk space:** kept for **7 days** while the server is critically low
  on space. A notice is shown in the trash bin while this applies.

Permanently deleted files can't be restored. See the Privacy Policy for what
happens to the data on disk.

### 3.4 File integrity checks

FluxDrop automatically calculates checksums of stored files so it can detect
damaged data. This only runs when the server isn't busy, and it doesn't
involve anyone looking at your files.

## 4. Sharing

You can create share links for files and folders. For each link you choose:

- an optional **expiry date**,
- whether visitors need a **FluxDrop account**,
- whether visitors may **upload** into a shared folder (anyone, or only
  signed-in users),
- whether files can be **previewed** in the browser or **embedded** on other
  sites,
- whether **access statistics** are recorded (on by default).

**Access statistics:** when they are on, you can see when your link was
opened and, for visitors who were signed in, their **username**. Please use
this information only to understand how your share is used. Don't use it to
track or harass people.

Anyone who has a share link can access what it points to. Treat links like
keys. If a link leaks, delete it or set it to expire.

Expired links stop working, but the files stay in your storage. If you delete
a shared folder, it can still be reached through its link while it is in the
trash bin. Delete the link as well to cut off access right away.

**You are responsible for the content you share**, and for content that other
people upload into folders you've opened for uploads.
